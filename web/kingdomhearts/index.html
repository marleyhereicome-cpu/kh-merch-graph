What this is

A fan-run catalog of Kingdom Hearts merchandise from around the world. It isn't a store and doesn't sell anything. Each item tells you what it is, whether it's officially licensed or fan-made, what it cost when it first came out, and where people actually find it now, with a link to where every detail was checked.

Most fans only ever see what their local online shops show them. Plenty of great merch never crosses a border or a language barrier: Japanese prize plush and lottery figures, overseas-only collabs, fan-made apparel. This is a place to run into it.

What is Kingdom Hearts?

Kingdom Hearts is a long-running action RPG series from Square Enix and Disney, weaving Disney's worlds and characters together with an original cast (and the occasional Final Fantasy face). It's been running since 2002 across more than a dozen games, and it's the reason this much merch exists in the first place. More on Wikipedia ↗

What's a shipping proxy?

Most Japanese shops and marketplaces (Mercari, Yahoo Auctions, the Square Enix store) only ship inside Japan. A shipping proxy is a paid service (Buyee and ZenMarket are two well-known ones) that buys the item on your behalf with a Japanese address, then forwards it to you. They add a service fee plus international shipping on top of the item's price. We're NOT affiliated with either. This isn't something we do ourselves; we just point you to where an item shows up and let you take it from there.

What does "original price" mean?

It's the price an item had when it was first released, in the currency of the country it was released in (approximate conversions are shown in brackets, using a fixed rate, not a live one). Lottery prizes show the price of one draw, since the prize itself was never sold. Resale prices today can be far above or below the original; the original price is there so you have something to compare against.

How is the information on this site checked?

Each item marked “verified” has had its price, release date and product code checked directly against at least one of: the maker’s official site, an authorized retailer, or reliable press (4Gamer, Famitsu and the like). When the official information is outdated or can only be confirmed indirectly, the item shows an “estimated” reference price and the basis is noted. Listing photos and prices from marketplaces (eBay, Mercari and so on) are never used as evidence.
